Why the slogan is “we move heat”

Your air conditioner does not make cold air.

It moves heat. That is the entire job, it is where the name on the truck comes from, and once you know it most of what goes wrong stops being mysterious.

  1. 1
    In summer, it moves heat out.

    Refrigerant picks up heat from the air inside your house, carries it outside, and dumps it out of the outdoor unit. The cold is just what is left behind.

  2. 2
    In winter, a heat pump runs it backwards.

    The same cycle reverses. Even on a cold day there is heat in the outside air, and the system pulls it in. That is why a heat pump costs less to run than strip heat.

  3. 3
    So a breakdown is one of three things.

    The heat stopped moving because of refrigerant, because of airflow, or because of electrical. Knowing which one before the parts come off the truck is the whole job.

“They primarily just move heat. Either from outside to inside, or inside to outside.”

FAQ

Questions worth asking any HVAC company.

What areas do you cover?

North Augusta and Aiken County on the South Carolina side, and Augusta, Martinez, Evans, Grovetown and the surrounding Georgia communities. If you are near the edge of that, call and ask — the answer is usually yes.

My system is not cooling. Is there anything to check before I call?

Three quick things. Check that the filter is not filthy, check that the breaker for the outdoor unit has not tripped, and check that the thermostat is calling for cool and its batteries are good.

One thing not to do: if you see ice on the refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, switch the system off and leave it off. Running it iced up is how a bad afternoon turns into a compressor.

Do you work on heat pumps as well as gas furnaces?

Both. Heat pumps, gas furnaces, air handlers, package units and split systems. Heat pumps are the majority of what is installed around here, and their winter faults — defrost problems, systems locked on emergency heat, reversing-valve trouble — are routine work.

Will you tell me if a repair is not worth doing?

Yes, with the reasoning. Age of the system, what the repair costs, what is likely to fail next, and what a replacement would run. You get the numbers and you make the call — there is no commission riding on the answer.

Do you handle full system replacements?

Yes. When replacement is genuinely the right move, the new system gets sized for the house rather than simply matched to whatever was there before. A system that is too large cools badly and leaves the house humid, which is its own kind of uncomfortable.

Do you take light commercial work?

Yes — small offices, retail spaces, shops and churches, including rooftop and package units. Larger industrial plants are outside what one technician should be promising to cover, so those get an honest referral instead.

Are you running HVAC training classes yet?

Not yet. A hands-on training lab is being built in the CSRA, and the service side of the business is what funds it. There are no dates, no tuition and no enrolment at this stage — but call and say you are interested in training, and your name goes on the list to be told first.

Hot house? Cold house? Strange noise?

Call and describe what it is doing. If it can be sorted out over the phone, you will be told that instead of being sold a service call.
